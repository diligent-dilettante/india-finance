# MSME and Udyam

Classification changes real money for an Indian company — payment protection, procurement access,
credit terms. It is not a compliance footnote.

> **Verify the thresholds before relying on them.** They have been revised more than once and the
> figures below carry the date they were current. Check the Udyam portal (`udyamregistration.gov.in`)
> or the latest Finance Act before computing a classification, exactly as the parent skill verifies
> tax slabs. If the user's registration certificate is available, it states the classification
> directly and that is better evidence than a computation.

---

## Classification

Based on **both** investment in plant and machinery **and** annual turnover. A company falls into the
higher category if it exceeds either limit.

**Thresholds as revised in Union Budget 2025, stated effective FY2025-26 — verify:**

| Tier | Investment | Turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | ≤ ₹2.5 Cr | ≤ ₹10 Cr |
| Small | ≤ ₹25 Cr | ≤ ₹100 Cr |
| Medium | ≤ ₹125 Cr | ≤ ₹500 Cr |

**Previous thresholds, effective from July 2020 — relevant for historical years:**

| Tier | Investment | Turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | ≤ ₹1 Cr | ≤ ₹5 Cr |
| Small | ≤ ₹10 Cr | ≤ ₹50 Cr |
| Medium | ≤ ₹50 Cr | ≤ ₹250 Cr |

Both sets matter: a company's status in FY2022 is determined by the rules then in force, which
affects whether an MSMED payment claim from that period stands.

**Notes on computing it:** exports are excluded from turnover for classification. Investment is
taken from the income tax return, and land and building are excluded from plant and machinery.
Classification is calculated on a group basis where entities have the same PAN.

## What Micro and Small status actually buys

### Payment protection — MSMED Act s.15–16

A buyer must pay a registered micro or small enterprise within **45 days**, or the agreed period if
shorter. Beyond that, compound interest accrues at **three times the RBI bank rate**, and it is not
deductible for the buyer's tax.

This is the most commercially significant benefit and it is widely under-used. Two practical points:

- The buyer's disclosure obligations mean overdue MSE payables appear in their own audited accounts
  and tax audit report, which creates real pressure.
- It applies to **micro and small only**, not medium.

**Where this shows up in analysis:** if the company under review is a *buyer*, check for MSE
payables running past 45 days — that is a quantifiable liability, not just a working-capital choice.
If it is a *seller*, overdue receivables from large buyers carry a statutory interest claim that
nobody has valued.

### Public procurement

Under the Public Procurement Policy for MSEs, registered micro and small enterprises typically get:

- **Exemption from Earnest Money Deposit** and tender fees
- **Purchase preference** — the ability to match the lowest price within a band and take a share of
  the order
- **Relaxation of prior turnover and experience criteria** in many central PSU tenders

The practical effect is that a small company can bid work its balance sheet alone would not support.
Verify against the specific tender document — implementation varies by buyer.

### Credit and other

Priority sector lending classification, collateral-free lending under CGTMSE up to the scheme limit,
and various state-level incentives. Interest subvention schemes appear and lapse — check currency
before relying on one.

---

## The strategic point worth surfacing

**These benefits are a function of being small, and they erode as the business grows.** A company
whose tender access depends on MSE exemptions is standing on scaffolding that comes down at the
turnover threshold.

That is rarely modelled. When a business is planning growth that would cross a tier boundary, the
loss of procurement preference and payment protection is a real cost of that growth and belongs in
the analysis. Flag it rather than leaving it to be discovered.

Conversely, a company sitting well inside Small with idle capacity has an asset most competitors
lack, and under-using it is a live commercial gap.

## Reading the certificate

A Udyam registration certificate states the registration number (format `UDYAM-XX-00-0000000`, where
`XX` is the state code), the classification, the declared investment and turnover, the NIC codes for
activities, and the date of registration.

Classification updates automatically from filed ITR and GST data, so the certificate may show a tier
the company no longer occupies. Check the date and reconcile against the current year's figures.
