# Who is actually using this

The default build assumes a salaried metro professional with a clean salary account and
separate cards. That person is well served. Several others are not, and the failures are
quiet rather than loud.

Identify which of these applies in the first session. Ask, don't infer from the
statements.

## Salaried, no dependents, metro

The default. Salary credit, rent, SIPs, cards. Everything works.

Watch: HRA is claimable only under the old regime, and for most people in this shape the
new regime still wins. Compute it, don't assume it.

## Salaried with a home loan and dependents

The old regime frequently wins here and the default assumption flips.

Needs: home loan interest under 24(b), principal inside 80C, school tuition fees also
inside 80C, HRA if renting in a different city from the owned property (which is allowed
and commonly missed).

**The principal and interest split is not in the bank statement.** The EMI is one debit.
Ask for the lender's annual interest certificate. Never estimate the split; it directly
sets the 24(b) claim.

## Freelancer, consultant or professional

**The largest gap in the default build.** No Form 16, no salary credit, income arriving
irregularly from several clients.

- **Presumptive taxation under 44ADA** applies to specified professions with gross
  receipts under the threshold: declare 50% of receipts as income, no books required.
  For many consultants this beats itemised deductions comfortably. Raise it; most people
  in this shape have not heard of it.
- **Advance tax is theirs to manage.** No employer is deducting. Four instalments,
  cumulative at 15/45/75/100 percent. Interest under 234B and 234C on shortfalls. Flag
  this early in the year, not in March.
- **TDS deducted by clients** shows in 26AS and is creditable. The bank statement shows
  the net figure, so gross income is understated unless 26AS is read alongside.
- Business expenses are deductible if itemising, which means some spending is not
  personal spending. Ask which categories are professional before reporting a savings
  rate that treats them as lifestyle.

## Running a family business or a shop

Two problems, both structural.

**Personal and business money share an account.** Extremely common and it breaks every
ratio. Do not attempt to untangle it heuristically. Ask which counterparties are
business, tag them, and report personal and business flows separately. If they cannot be
separated, say the analysis is unreliable rather than producing a confident wrong number.

**Cash is a large share of turnover.** The visibility warning matters more here. Above
roughly 10% of outflow in cash, state the size of the blind spot every time, not once.

Business finance itself stays out of scope. Their personal exposure to it does not: the
stake, the drawings, and any personal guarantee behind business debt.

## Senior citizen or retired parent

Different slabs and different deductions. Getting this wrong overstates their tax
materially.

- Old regime basic exemption is higher with age: **₹3,00,000 for 60 and above**,
  **₹5,00,000 for 80 and above**. The new regime does not vary by age.
- **80TTB** allows up to ₹50,000 of interest income deduction for 60 and above, replacing
  80TTA. Retired people often hold large FDs, so this is not marginal.
- Income is pension, interest and rent rather than salary. Standard deduction applies to
  pension.
- Advance tax is not payable by a resident senior citizen with no business income.

## Supporting parents or an extended family

Regular transfers to parents or siblings are normal and are neither spending nor saving
in any useful sense. Give them their own category rather than burying them in
discretionary, where they distort every discretionary comparison.

**80D has a separate, higher limit for parents.** Premiums paid for senior-citizen
parents are deductible on top of the taxpayer's own limit. People pay these premiums and
routinely fail to claim them.

Medical expenditure for very senior parents without insurance may also qualify. Ask.

## First job, no assets

Degrade gracefully. Do not open an asset register for someone with a savings account and
one card, and do not compute allocation across a portfolio that does not exist.

Cash flow, a savings rate, and one recommendation is the whole useful output. Offer the
rest when there is something to allocate.

## NRE and NRO accounts

If either appears, stop and say the residency question is out of scope here. Tax
treatment turns on residential status under the Income Tax Act and on FEMA rules, and
getting it wrong is expensive. Route to a CA who handles non-resident cases.

Do not attempt a regime comparison on non-resident income.

## Agricultural income

Exempt, but it is aggregated for rate-determination purposes when it exceeds ₹5,000
alongside other income above the exemption limit. Flag it and route to a CA rather than
computing it.

## What to ask in the first session

Four questions, and they take a minute:

1. Salaried, self-employed, business, or retired?
2. Home loan, rent, or neither?
3. Anyone financially dependent on you, and do you pay premiums for parents?
4. Is any of this account business money?

The answers change the tax computation, the categorisation and the tone of the report.
Ask once, write them to `profile.json`, and never ask again.
